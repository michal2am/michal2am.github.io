About

I am a computational biophysicist. I study how membrane proteins of the brain turn structure into function, combining molecular dynamics, homology modelling, molecular docking and kinetic modelling with experimental data - mostly structural biology and electrophysiology.

My current project, STARGATE, looks at the water channel aquaporin-4 and the potassium channel Kir4.1 at astrocyte endfeet, and at how they regulate water transport and intracranial pressure.

My earlier work concerned the GABA type A receptor, the main inhibitory receptor of the adult brain and a target of anxiolytic, anticonvulsant, antidepressant and anaesthetic drugs: how it binds its agonist, how its subunits and domains interact, and how the channel gates.

I received my PhD in biological sciences from the University of Wrocław in 2022 (thesis awarded with distinction, supervised by Prof. Jerzy W. Mozrzymas). Before that I completed an MSc Eng in Biotechnology (Bioinformatics, 2015) and a BSc Eng in Biomedical Engineering (2014) at Wrocław University of Science and Technology. I previously worked in the Department of Biophysics and Neuroscience at Wrocław Medical University and was a visiting researcher at the Science for Life Laboratory in Stockholm.

Methods and collaboration

I work with a broad set of molecular modelling tools for proteins, ligands and membranes: all-atom and coarse-grained molecular dynamics of membrane systems (GROMACS, CHARMM36, Martini 3, CHARMM-GUI), structure prediction and homology modelling (AlphaFold, Boltz, Modeller), protein–ligand and protein–protein docking (AutoDock Vina, GNINA, HADDOCK), pKa estimation and constant-pH simulations, and kinetic modelling of ion-channel activity. Analysis is scripted in Python and Bash.

I am open to collaboration with experimental groups working on protein structure-function relationship or protein-protein, protein-ligand or protein-membrane interactions. If a structural or dynamic model could help explain your data, or your data could test a model, write to me.

Ongoing

STARGATE

Scaffold-Tethered Aquaporin Regulation at the Gliovascular Astrocyte Terminal Endfeet

The brain is about 80% water, and its exchange between blood and nervous tissue is controlled at astrocyte endfeet, the specialised processes that wrap brain capillaries. Two membrane proteins are concentrated there: the water channel aquaporin-4 (AQP4) and the potassium channel Kir4.1, both anchored by the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC). When this system fails, the result can be brain oedema, hydrocephalus or idiopathic intracranial hypertension.

STARGATE uses computational biology to explain, at the molecular level, how these proteins regulate water transport and intracranial pressure. It builds on cryo-EM structures published in 2024–2025 that have not yet been studied in depth by simulation.

  • AQP4. Dynamics of the M1, M23 and ex isoforms; how orthogonal arrays of particles form; the roles of palmitoylation and membrane composition.
  • Kir4.1. Interaction with PIP2 and other membrane lipids; the mechanism of pH modulation; effects of EAST/SeSAME syndrome mutations; a structural model of the Kir4.1/Kir5.1 heteromer.
  • Long term. Interactions between AQP4, Kir4.1 and DAPC, and a multiscale model linking molecular dynamics with brain haemodynamics.
Methods
All-atom (CHARMM36) and coarse-grained (Martini 3) molecular dynamics in GROMACS, constant-pH simulations, homology modelling, docking, AI-based structure prediction

Past

Global gating of the GABAA receptor: Φ-value analysis

The first comprehensive Φ-value (REFER) analysis of the GABAA receptor, covering point mutations in many domains of the protein. The method was originally developed for protein folding; we adapted it to describe the activation of a ligand-gated ion channel. The results show that gating is highly synchronised: the extracellular and transmembrane domains move in tight coordination, rather than passing a conformational change along step by step. Ligand binding depends on local interactions, gating on the whole molecule. The modelling and analysis methods are implemented as reusable Python scripts.

Agonist binding and proton modulation at the GABA binding site

Identification and characterisation of key residues at the orthosteric binding site, in particular β2E155 and β2E153. β2E155 acts as a proton sensor whose protonation depends on the receptor state, which explains how pH modulates the receptor. β2E153 stabilises the bound agonist through the interaction between loops B and C. The work combined homology modelling, molecular dynamics and pKa estimation with patch-clamp electrophysiology.

Interactions between subunits and domains of the GABAA receptor

A molecular description of protein–protein interactions inside this heteropentamer. At the interface between the extracellular and transmembrane domains, β2V53 and α1P277 form part of a lock with the M2–M3 loop that carries the gating signal towards the pore. At the top of the extracellular domain, the α1F14–β2F31 pair holds neighbouring subunits together above the binding site. Computational modelling was backed throughout by electrophysiological recordings.
